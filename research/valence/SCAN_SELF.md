**Two caveats on what this measures here, both load-bearing.**

*It is discontinuity, not contradiction.* That reading requires a working
sign channel. With 1.1% of adjacency edges negative (§1), almost every
edge asserts agreement, so a high residual means "this segment reads
differently from its neighbours", not "this segment conflicts with them".

*Seeded nodes are structurally favoured.* A pinned node cannot relax toward
its neighbours, so it carries residual by construction — frustration is
part signal, part seed-detector. The two lists below are therefore split:
the unseeded one is what the graph found on its own, and it is the list
worth reading first.

## 6. What this run does not establish

- The lexicon is hand-authored (see `lexicon.en.tsv` header). Validating
  the field against it would be circular. There is no external criterion
  in this run — unlike Tier 1, which had one and reported r=+0.941.
- Segments are split by regex. `--dump-segments` is the only way to know
  whether the units being scored are the units you think they are.
- Line numbers locate the PARAGRAPH a segment came from, not the segment.
  For a module docstring that means every segment reports line 1.
- Source files contribute comments only; identifiers and code are dropped.
- Boilerplate is not detected. `LICENSE` scores negative on *damages*,
  *liability*, *without warranty*; it is legal text, not an opinion. Any
  corpus with vendored licences or CoCs will show the same artefact.
- Files needing an extractor (pdf, docx, ipynb) were skipped entirely and
  are listed in §1. Their absence is a coverage gap, not a null result.
